Problem, due to Will Sawin.

Let X be a finite bijective set-theoretic solution of the Yang-Baxter
equation, with braid actions

  rho^X_n : B_n -> Sym(X^n).

A finite rack Y is viewed as the set-theoretic solution

  R_Y(a,b) = (a*b, a).

Question.  Does every finite bijective set-theoretic YBE solution X admit a
single finite rack Y, independent of n, such that

  ker rho^Y_n <= ker rho^X_n

for every n?

Known closed cases and guardrails.

1. Finite one-sided nondegenerate solutions are closed by the derived
   rack/guitar construction, with kernel equality in every arity.

2. Involutive solutions, products of dominated solutions, and braided
   quotients of dominated solutions are dominated.

3. A genuinely degenerate X cannot be solved by a finite nondegenerate
   braided cover.  A coordinatewise rack quotient is also too restrictive.

4. No single braid is invisible to all finite racks: Artin faithfulness and
   residual finiteness of free groups give a finite conjugation rack detecting
   any fixed nontrivial braid.

5. Fixed-arity rack cofinality is available: for every fixed k and every
   finite quotient representation theta:B_k -> H, there exists a finite rack
   Z with ker rho^Z_k <= ker theta.  Hence any counterexample must force
   failures at unbounded braid index.

Transparent extension.

For a rack Y define Y^0=Y sqcup {0} by

  a*b = old a*b for a,b in Y,
  0*b = b,
  a*0 = 0,
  0*0 = 0.

This is a rack.  Let T_2 be the two-element trivial rack.  Set

  Q = Y^0 x T_2.

The T_2 factor forces Q-invisible braids to be pure.  The transparent color
gives exact support control: deleting the transparent strands realizes the
deleted braid action on Y.

Ordinary-Brunnian reduction.

Write Brun_n for the ordinary Brunnian subgroup:

  Brun_n = {
    beta in P_n :
    partial_I beta = 1 for every proper I subsetneq {1,...,n}
  }.

For every finite rack Y, the rack Q=Y^0 x T_2 dominates X if and only if

  Brun_n cap ker rho^Q_n <= ker rho^X_n

for every n.

Sketch of the nontrivial direction: if Q fails, first choose a minimal-arity
Q-invisible, X-visible witness whose proper deletions are Q- and X-invisible.
Then use deletion maps d_i:P_n->P_{n-1} and trivial-strand insertions
s_i:P_{n-1}->P_n.  With

  K_m = ker rho^Q_m cap ker rho^X_m,

multiply the witness by inserted copies s_i(d_i beta)^{-1}, killing
one-strand deletions from right to left.  The face identities

  d_i s_i = id,
  d_i s_j = s_{j-1} d_i for i<j,
  d_i s_j = s_j d_{i-1} for i>j

preserve already killed faces.  Since all proper deletions of the current
witness lie in K and insertion preserves K, the X- and Q-actions do not
change.  The final witness is an ordinary Brunnian braid, still Q-invisible
and X-visible.

Cofinal negative target.

Let R_1,R_2,... enumerate finite rack isomorphism classes, set

  P_m = R_1 x ... x R_m,
  Q_m = P_m^0 x T_2.

If no finite rack dominates X, then for every m there are n_m -> infinity and

  beta_m in Brun_{n_m}

such that

  rho^{Q_m}_{n_m}(beta_m)=1,
  rho^X_{n_m}(beta_m) != 1.

Uniformly nilpotent class closed.

Let

  F_{n-1} = ker(d_n:P_n -> P_{n-1}),

the free group generated by A_{1n},...,A_{n-1,n}.  If there is a constant c
such that rho^X_n(F_{n-1}) is nilpotent of class at most c for every n, then
X is dominated by a finite rack.

Proof sketch: fixed-arity rack cofinality handles n<=c+1.  For n>c+1, every
ordinary Brunnian beta lies in F_{n-1} and, because killing any generator
A_{in} kills beta, lies in

  intersection_i <<A_{in}>> <= gamma_{n-1}(F_{n-1}).

The inclusion follows from the Magnus expansion: a nonzero term of degree
< n-1 omits some variable and survives after killing it.  Since the image of
F_{n-1} is nilpotent of class <=c<n-1, beta is X-trivial.  The finite rack
dominator supplied by this argument is Q=Y_0^0 x T_2 for the finite product
Y_0 of the fixed-arity racks, not necessarily Y_0 itself.
