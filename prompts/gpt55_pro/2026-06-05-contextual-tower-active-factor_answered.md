# Contextual Tower Or Active Factor Prompt

Status: answered on 2026-06-05.

Follow-up status:

GPT-5.5 Pro confirmed that the finite-state contextual tower `C_M(X)` is the
right strengthening of `C_L(X)`, but it still does not close Sawin without a
uniform finite/profinite lemma.  Pointwise separation by finer finite state
quotients is not enough: Sawin requires one finite state quotient `M` and one
finite rack quotient working for every arity.

The answer identified three independent barriers: finite-state uniformity,
contextual scattering cycles surviving all finite `M`, and nonclosed
stabilizer cosets in `As(C_M(X))`.  The next active prompt asks whether this
uniform tower lemma can be proved for residual rigid cores, refuted by a
realizable tower-equivalence obstruction, or replaced by a finite automaton /
escape-holonomy detector theorem.

Prompt:

```text
Please answer self-containedly and mathematically. Do not give only a bounded
computation. We are working on Sawin's finite rack-domination problem:

    For every finite bijective set-theoretic YBE solution X, does there exist
    a finite rack Y such that ker rho_n^Y <= ker rho_n^X for every n?

Write r_X(x,y)=(L_x(y),R_y(x)).

Known guardrails:

1. Product observers are inert but kernel-universal by singular padding:
   X=E x Z has ker rho_n^X=ker rho_n^Z.

2. One-coordinate prefix rack labels collapse through evaluation:

       psi(p,x)=psi(1,p(x)).

   Thus the one-sided route through W_L(X) needs normalized-prefix
   orbit-faithfulness and profinite stabilizer separability.

3. The two-sided contextual rack C_L(X) is valid and stronger.  Let S=S_L^1.
   It has generators

       c_{p,x,q}        (p,q in S, x in X)

   and relations, for r_X(x,y)=(u,v),

       c_{p,u,L_v q}=c_{pL_x,y,q},                              (C1)
       c_{pL_u,v,q}=c_{pL_x,y,q} triangleright c_{p,x,L_y q}.   (C2)

   With

       p_i=L_{x_1}...L_{x_i},       p_0=1,
       q_i=L_{x_{i+1}}...L_{x_n},   q_n=1,

   the labels

       Theta_n(w)=
       (c_{p_0,x_1,q_1},...,c_{p_{n-1},x_n,q_n})

   are B_n-equivariant.  If a finite quotient of C_L(X) separates distinct
   points inside every braid orbit, then X is dominated by that finite rack.

4. C_L(X) has a scattering normalization.  For a word a=z_1...z_k, define
   L_a=L_{z_1}...L_{z_k} and recursively

       sigma_empty(x)=x,        Delta_empty(x)=1,
       sigma_{az}(x)=sigma_a(L_z(x)),
       Delta_{az}(x)=Delta_a(L_z(x)) L_{R_x(z)}.

   Then

       c_{L_a,x,q}=c_{1,L_a(x),Delta_a(x)q}.

   If L_a=L_{a'}, then C_L(X) identifies

       c_{1,L_a(x),Delta_a(x)q}
       =
       c_{1,L_a(x),Delta_{a'}(x)q}.

   Thus C_L(X) is stronger than W_L(X) but still loses word-level escape data.

5. A finite local contextual obstruction can occur.  Let ~_sc be generated by
   C1 moves

       (p,u,L_vq) ~_sc (pL_x,y,q).

   If for a nontrivial crossing r_X(x,y)=(u,v),

       (p,x,L_yq) ~_sc (pL_x,y,q) ~_sc (pL_u,v,q),

   then the local braid generator gives a same-Theta collision.  A concrete
   pattern forcing this is

       r_X(a,y)=(x,y),
       r_X(x,y)=(u,y),
       u != x,
       pL_a=pL_x=pL_u.

6. The profinite obstruction is also exact.  Let G_C(X)=As(C_L(X)) and
   H_alpha=Stab(c_alpha).  If c_beta=g.c_alpha, then

       alpha ==_{C,fin} beta
       iff
       g in closure_prof(H_alpha).

7. Failure of contextual separation does not automatically yield a
   domination-reducing active factor.  The collapse depends on (p,q), not on a
   coordinatewise YBE congruence.  A state-dependent active factor would require
   a finite YBE solution Z and maps pi_{p,q}:X->Z such that

       r_Z(pi_{p,L_yq}(x), pi_{pL_x,q}(y))
       =
       (pi_{p,L_vq}(u), pi_{pL_u,q}(v)).

8. Escape holonomy factors through C_L(X) only under rack determinacy by
   (p,x,q): there must be a finite rack E and epsilon:S x X x S -> E satisfying
   the C1/C2 equations.  If escape labels distinguish data beyond (p,x,q), or
   use a non-rack crossing law, they do not factor through C_L(X).

Possible stronger detector.

Let M be a finite quotient of the left structure monoid generated by symbols
lambda_x with relations

    lambda_x lambda_y = lambda_u lambda_v

whenever r_X(x,y)=(u,v).  Define C_M(X) with generators

    d_{a,x,b}        (a,b in M, x in X)

and relations

    d_{a,u,lambda_v b}=d_{a lambda_x,y,b},
    d_{a lambda_u,v,b}
      =
    d_{a lambda_x,y,b} triangleright d_{a,x,lambda_y b}.

The same proof gives braid-equivariant labels.  Taking M=S_L^1 recovers
C_L(X).  Finer finite monoid quotients may retain word-level escape data that
S_L^1 loses.

Task.

2. Does failure of C_L(X)-separation imply that some finer finite-state

3. Can the full marked-word contextual rack be shown residually finite on the

5. Compare the contextual tower with escape-rack/minimal-ideal holonomy.  Does
   escape-holonomy separation imply finite separation in some C_M(X) under a

   that escape holonomy can retain?

6. Determine whether residual rigid hypotheses rule out:
   - finite contextual scattering cycles;
   - same-orbit Theta collisions in all finite C_M(X);
   - orbit-relevant nonclosed stabilizer cosets in As(C_M(X)).

7. State the strongest valid reduced theorem.  Useful outcomes include:
   - a contextual tower finite separation theorem implying Sawin;
   - a contextual failure -> active factor theorem;
   - an escape determinacy theorem implying a finite rack quotient;
   - or a realizable obstruction showing the contextual tower route cannot
     close Sawin.

Important:

- Do not claim product observers alone prove domination.
- Do not use ordinary quotients as active factors unless ker rho_n^Z <=
  ker rho_n^X is proved.
- Do not replace profinite subgroup separability with finite semigroup closure.
- Do not claim C_L(X) retains word-level escape data after quotienting by
  S_L^1; it has the scattering collapse above.
- A useful answer should either prove a contextual tower/active-factor/escape
  theorem, refute it with a realizable obstruction, or isolate the exact next
  missing finite/profinite lemma.
```
