# Normalized-law attempt audit

Date: 2026-05-28

This note records a tempting but insufficient route toward counterexample B.

## Exponent laws

For any integer `N`, the free word

```text
x^lcm(1,...,N)
```

is a law for every finite group whose exponent divides `lcm(1,...,N)`.  In
particular, every group of order at most `N` satisfies this word, because every
element order is at most the group order.

The code in `src/ybe_domination/group_laws.py` verifies these laws on explicit
finite groups and connects them to the two-strand pure braid powers

```text
(sigma_1^2)^lcm(1,...,N).
```

For two strands, the recursive Artin longitudes of these powers are exponent
law words in the meridians, so the finite-G longitude detector misses them for
all groups whose relevant exponents divide `lcm(1,...,N)`.
In the subgroup notation of `proofs/longitude_subgroup_profile.md`, this says
`V_beta(G)` is the trivial subgroup for those groups.

## Why this is not B

The same exponent trick also kills any fixed finite action generated by a
single pure braid.  If a finite YBE solution has finite action image and a
pure braid `p` acts with order `m`, then

```text
p^lcm(1,...,N)
```

acts trivially for every `N >= m`.

Thus a counterexample cannot be produced merely by taking larger and larger
powers of one braid whose longitudes become exponent laws.  A real normalized
law obstruction would need a sequence whose Artin longitudes become universal
finite-group laws while the braid words remain nontrivial in the fixed finite
YBE action.  That requires a more structured construction than exponent
powers.

## Executable audit

`tools/run_law_shortcut_audit.py` applies this to the one-colour two-point
permutation interval.  The pure braid `sigma_1^2` has residual action order
`2`.  Once `lcm(1,...,N)` is even, the exponent-law braid no longer moves the
interval, even though it becomes invisible to more finite groups.

The generated report is `proofs/law_shortcut_audit.json`.
