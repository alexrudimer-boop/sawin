# Fixed-variety barrier

Date: 2026-05-28

This note records the precise obstruction that a moving normalized-law B
sequence must overcome.  It is a theorem-level guardrail, not a proof of the
Master Local-Minimal Residual Theorem.

## Varieties Of Fixed Finite Groups

For a finite group `G`, let `var(G)` denote the group variety generated by
`G`: the class of all groups satisfying every group word identity that holds
in `G`.

Equivalently, for a finite group `H`,

```text
H in var(G)
```

if and only if every free word that is a law on `G` is also a law on `H`.
This is the standard Birkhoff variety theorem specialized to groups.

## Barrier Lemma

Let `X` be a fixed finite YBE solution.  Suppose the law-braid construction
uses braid indices `q_j`, standard last-strand pure generators, and their
images

```text
H_j = < rho_{X,q_j}(A_{1,q_j}), ..., rho_{X,q_j}(A_{q_j-1,q_j}) >
      <= Sym(X^{q_j}).
```

If there is one finite group `G_0` such that

```text
H_j in var(G_0)
```

for all sufficiently large `j`, then no normalized-law sequence invisible to
`G_0` can move through these groups.

Indeed, if `w_j` is eventually a law on every fixed finite group, then in
particular it is eventually a law on `G_0`.  Since `H_j in var(G_0)`, the
same word `w_j` is a law on `H_j`.  Therefore

```text
w_j(rho(A_{1,q_j}),...,rho(A_{q_j-1,q_j})) = 1,
```

so the embedded braid acts trivially on `X^{q_j}` through this pure-image
subgroup.

The same statement applies orbit-locally or residually: if the relevant
moving holonomy groups all lie in `var(G_0)`, then law-longitude invisibility
to `G_0` kills that moving holonomy.

## Consequence For B

A normalized-law counterexample cannot be certified by showing only that the
finite action images grow.  It must show finite-variety escape:

> For every finite group `G_0`, infinitely many relevant moving holonomy
> groups fail some law of `G_0`.

Equivalently, after choosing an increasing list of all finite groups up to
size `j`, the group or assigned pure-generator tuple used at degree `q_j`
must admit a word that is a law on that entire finite list but evaluates
nontrivially on the YBE action.

This is the exact condition phrased in
`proofs/moving_variety_counterexample_criterion.md` and localized to
structure orbits in `proofs/structure_orbit_law_obstruction.md`.

## Consequence For A

The A-route may be proved branchwise by showing that all residual moving
holonomy groups lie in the variety generated by one fixed finite detector
group built from the interval:

```text
G(pi,Q) =
  product of product-label groups,
  Green kernel-block groups,
  Schutzenberger groups,
  affine/cyclic/known branch factors,
  and quotient detector factors.
```

This is weaker than requiring the moving groups themselves to be subgroups or
quotients of `G(pi,Q)`, but strong enough for normalized-law obstruction
control.  To finish the sharp obstruction theorem one still needs the
stronger finite-longitude factorization: the residual labels must be forced
by recursive Artin-longitude evaluations in that fixed group, not merely by
abstract word laws.

Thus the fixed-variety barrier narrows the B-route and clarifies why it does
not by itself prove A.

## Executable Diagnostic

The helper

```text
short_law_escaping_variety(G,H,arity,max_length)
```

searches for a bounded-length law of `G` that fails on `H`.  A returned word
is a genuine finite certificate that `H` is not in `var(G)`.  A missing word
is only bounded evidence and must not be cited as proof of membership in
`var(G)`.

The regression tests record two convention checks:

- `S_3` escapes the abelian variety generated by `C_6` via the commutator
  word;
- `C_3`, a subgroup of `S_3`, has no bounded escape from `var(S_3)` in the
  checked window, as expected.
