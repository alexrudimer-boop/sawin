# Product longitude-subgroup criterion

Date: 2026-05-28

This note strengthens the product-label target.  It is not the missing
all-`n` product theorem, but it replaces a too-narrow diagnostic with the
right finite-group verbal subgroup condition.

## Setup

Let `pi:X -> Z` be a product-permutation local interval, either swapped or
direct.  Let `H_prod` be the finite totalized product-label group on the
tagged fibre union, as in `proofs/product_label_group_detector.md`.

For a braid degree `n`, a base colour tuple `z`, and a base-fixed,
strand-permutation-trivial braid `beta`, the product normal form gives closed
labels

```text
h_{z,j}(beta) in H_prod.
```

These are computed by

```text
product_closed_label_permutations(interval, branch, z, beta).
```

## Longitude-Value Subgroup

For the same finite group `H_prod`, define

```text
S_beta(H_prod)
  =
{ phi(L_i(beta)) : phi:F_n -> H_prod, 1 <= i <= n }.
```

Let

```text
V_beta(H_prod) = < S_beta(H_prod) > <= H_prod
```

be the subgroup generated by all such longitude values.

The key sufficient condition is:

```text
h_{z,j}(beta) in V_beta(H_prod)   for every closed product label.
```

If this holds for every `n,z,j,beta` in the product branch, then finite
`H_prod` longitude identity kills the product residual action.  Indeed,
`Lambda_{H_prod,n}(beta)=Lambda_{H_prod,n}(1)` says every element of
`S_beta(H_prod)` is the identity, so the generated subgroup
`V_beta(H_prod)` collapses to the identity.  Hence every closed label in that
subgroup is identity.

This is exactly the "finite product of longitude evaluations" form of
`proofs/label_longitude_factorization.md`.  It is strictly more flexible than
asking each closed label to equal one longitude value under one assignment.

## Executable Audit

The helper

```text
product_closed_label_longitude_subgroup_audit(interval, branch, z, beta)
```

performs the finite fixed-word check:

1. computes all recursive Artin longitudes of `beta`;
2. evaluates every longitude under every assignment `F_n -> H_prod`;
3. generates the subgroup of `H_prod` from those values;
4. checks whether each nonidentity closed product label lies in that subgroup.

The helper records:

- the number of distinct longitude-value generators;
- the size of the generated subgroup;
- one row for each nonidentity closed label, with a membership flag.

The companion bounded scanner

```text
product_closed_label_longitude_subgroup_failures(...)
```

searches a supplied list of braid words for closed labels outside this
subgroup.  Such a failure would be a concrete product-branch obstruction
target, though it would still need to be promoted to an all-finite-group
normalized-law sequence before it could prove outcome B.

`tests/test_product_permutation.py` verifies the strengthened criterion on
the current two-colour identity-base cyclic fixture.  That example already
had per-label witnesses, but the subgroup audit is the more faithful
formulation for future coloured product candidates.  The same test file also
checks that the bounded subgroup-failure scanner finds no failure on that
fixture through the sampled braid words.

The generated audit `proofs/product_subgroup_audit.md` applies the same
criterion to all two-colour/two-point-fibre local-minimal product branches
over every two-point quotient YBE base.  In that corpus the largest
`H_prod` has order `24`, so all assignments `F_3 -> H_prod` are enumerated
exactly for every closed word in the bounded scan.  The audit finds `128`
closed nonidentity product-label words and `0` longitude-subgroup failures.
This remains finite candidate evidence only; it is included to make the
product B search target more concrete.

The helper

```text
product_closed_label_exact_audit(interval, branch, groups, z)
```

provides a complementary exact fixed-degree check.  Instead of scanning words
up to a length bound, it closes the finite joint image of the product-label
action on the single colour tuple `z`, a base detector action, the Artin
strand permutation, and the listed finite-group longitude detector states.
The base detector defaults to the quotient colour solution, but can be
supplied explicitly as the dominating rack `Q` or any stronger finite
detector.  A nontruncated no-failure row proves the closed-label kernel
implication for that fixed tuple, base detector, and finite-group detector
list.  It does not prove the subgroup membership lemma for all braid degrees,
but it removes word-length cutoffs from small product detector tests.

The generated audit `proofs/product_exact_closed_label_audit.md` runs this
exact check through all two-colour/two-point-fibre local-minimal product
branches at braid degree `2`.  It reports `368` fixed colour-tuple scans, `0`
truncations, and `0` untagged failures.  The raw product label group detector
does fail in `16` known rows already tagged by nondegenerate or
rack/nondegenerate branches, which confirms that the product branch theorem
must allow multiplication by the known detector factors named in the target
lemma.

A regression fixture records the first such known-row failure.  On that
two-colour non-identity quotient row, the raw product label group has order
`4` and is blind to the closed label carried by `sigma_1^4` at the fixed tuple
`(0,0)`.  Multiplying the detector list by `S_4`, a finite known-branch
symmetric detector on the four total points, removes that fixed-degree
failure.  This is not an all-`n` proof of the known branch; it is a guardrail
showing that exact product audits must compose detector factors instead of
treating the product label group as the whole local detector.

The generic helper

```text
subgroup_generated_elements(group, generators)
```

is in `src/ybe_domination/finite_group.py`; it closes a finite subgroup
inside an arbitrary explicit finite group.

The subgroup criterion is stable under fixed finite group maps.  The note
`proofs/longitude_subgroup_functoriality.md` proves that for any homomorphism
`f:G -> H`,

```text
f(V_beta(G)) <= V_beta(H),
```

with equality when `f` is surjective.  Therefore a product-label proof may
work upstairs in the totalized label group and push labels to a normalized
holonomy quotient, or work in a detector product and project to a factor,
without changing the braid index or the fixed detector group.

## A-Route Target

The product branch can now be stated without choosing individual witnesses:

> Product longitude-subgroup lemma.  For every local-minimal product
> interval, every braid degree `n`, every closed base tuple `z`, every
> residual braid `beta`, and every coordinate `j`, the closed product label
> `h_{z,j}(beta)` lies in `V_beta(H_prod)`, possibly after multiplying
> `H_prod` by the already-known cyclic, affine, coboundary, or known-branch
> detector factors.

If this lemma is proved, `A_{H_prod}` or the corresponding detector product
dominates the product interval for all braid degrees.  The group `H_prod`
depends only on the finite interval, not on `n`.

Before this theorem is applied, product local-minimality can now be certified
without enumerating all partition families.  The pair-closure helpers in
`src/ybe_domination/product_permutation.py` close each fibre pair under the
finite product-label groupoid; zero non-universal closures is exactly the
primitive/local-minimal condition for the product branch.  Thus a future
product counterexample must pass this explicit pair-closure gate before its
closed holonomy labels are relevant to the master theorem.

## B-Route Target

A product counterexample is also sharper now.  It must produce product data
and braids `beta_j in B_{q_j}` such that every finite group sees trivial
longitude data eventually, while some closed label stays outside the identity
despite the collapse of all longitude-value subgroups.

Equivalently, for the fixed product-label group of the interval, a finite
diagnostic obstruction should first appear as a closed label not lying in
`V_beta(H_prod)`.  Such a failure would not yet prove B, but it would be a
much more serious obstruction than the earlier failure of a common assignment
or a single-longitude witness.

The helper `product_closed_label_longitude_route_audit()` now reports all
three fixed-word routes at once: common assignment count, per-label
single-longitude witnesses, and subgroup membership.  Its role is bookkeeping,
not proof.  It makes a product branch audit falsifiable at the right point:
rows outside `V_beta(H_prod)` are the only fixed-word failures that can feed a
normalized-law B route, while rows merely lacking a common assignment are
already known to be harmless.

## Relation to the Master Gap

The same subgroup formulation applies beyond product labels.  In the
Green/corridor branch, the desired statement is that every residual holonomy
element lies in the subgroup generated by all recursive-longitude values in
the fixed two-sided Green detector product.  This input-dependent
longitude-subgroup formulation is the current cleanest target for the
bi-free universal-corridor bottleneck.

The product target can now also be expressed after gauge normalization.  The
helpers recorded in `proofs/product_holonomy_normalization.md` evaluate a
formal product-label word as `g_t f g_s^{-1}` in the finite product holonomy
group left by the coboundary audit.  For closed residual product paths, the
gauge transport itself telescopes to the identity, so this normalized holonomy
is the only part that can move a fibre.  Thus the genuinely coloured product
theorem may be proved either in the totalized group `H_prod` above or in this
smaller finite holonomy group, provided the same all-`n` longitude-subgroup
membership is proved.

The executable normalized check is
`product_holonomy_longitude_subgroup_audit()`.  It is the preferred diagnostic
for future genuinely coloured product candidates because coboundary transport
has already been removed before the finite-G longitude subgroup is tested.
