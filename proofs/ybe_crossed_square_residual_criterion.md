# YBE crossed-square residual criterion

Date: 2026-06-03

The Brunnian derivative gate reduces the high-arity obstruction search to the
residual double-deletion quotient

```text
R_{p,q}^I =
V_{p,q}^{I,Br} /
< nabla_q V_p^{I,Br},
  nabla_p V_q^{I,Br},
  M_{p,q}^I,
  Pi^sharp V_{p,q,B}^{I,Br} >.
```

The next structural refinement is that this quotient is governed by the
crossed square attached to the two deletion directions `p` and `q`.

## Crossed Square

Over a fixed survivor labelled object, the two deletion directions give finite
vertical bisection groups

```text
L -> M
L -> N
M -> P
N -> P.
```

Here `P` is the survivor bisection group, `M` and `N` are the one-deletion
bisection groups, and `L` is the two-deletion bisection group.  The maps are
induced by restriction along the deletion faces, and the remaining group acts
by transport.  The deletion-square coherence supplies the Peiffer pairing

```text
h: M x N -> L.
```

Equivalently, there is a canonical mutual subgroup generated by the image of
the nonabelian tensor product over `P`,

```text
kappa: M boxtimes_P N -> L.
```

Thus the "bounded mutual two-strand" term in the derivative gate is not an
ad hoc local correction.  It is the Peiffer image of the deletion crossed
square.

## Residual Quotient

For the Brunnian two-deletion layer, set

```text
L = V_{p,q}^{I,Br},
M = V_p^{I,Br},
N = V_q^{I,Br}.
```

Let

```text
partial: L -> M x N
```

be the pair of one-face restrictions.  Its kernel

```text
K = ker(partial)
```

is the square-internal Moore kernel: bisections invisible on both deletion
faces.

Let the edge-degenerate subgroup be

```text
E_{p,q} =
< nabla_q V_p^{I,Br},
  nabla_p V_q^{I,Br} >^L,
```

let `M_{p,q}` be the normal closure of the Peiffer mutual image
`im kappa`, and let

```text
B_{p,q} = Pi^sharp V_{p,q,B}^{I,Br}
```

be the fixed-base pullback subgroup.  Define

```text
D_{p,q} = < E_{p,q}, M_{p,q}, B_{p,q} >^L.
```

Then the residual quotient is

```text
R_{p,q}^I = L / D_{p,q}.
```

The map `partial` gives the exact sequence

```text
1 -> K/(K cap D_{p,q})
  -> R_{p,q}^I
  -> partial L / partial D_{p,q}
  -> 1.
```

So the residual quotient vanishes if and only if both the boundary quotient
and the Moore quotient vanish.

## Two Obstruction Groups

The boundary obstruction is

```text
H_partial^square(I;p,q|B) =
partial L / partial D_{p,q}.
```

It detects a Brunnian two-deletion bisection whose one-face boundary is not
produced by edge derivatives, Peiffer mutual terms, or fixed-base pullback.

The Moore-Peiffer obstruction is

```text
H_2^square(I;p,q|B) =
K/(K cap D_{p,q}).
```

It detects a square-internal Brunnian vertical cycle whose two one-face
boundaries are both trivial but which is still not generated by edge,
Peiffer, or base terms.

These two quotients are the exact positive and negative targets.

## Peiffer-Complete Vanishing

A compact sufficient theorem is:

```text
partial L = partial E_{p,q}
and
K <= < im kappa, B_{p,q} >^L
imply
R_{p,q}^I = 1.
```

The first condition says every Brunnian boundary is already the boundary of an
edge derivative.  The second says every square-internal Moore cycle is
generated by Peiffer mutual cycles and fixed-base pullback cycles.

This is the finite-operator positive route in its sharp crossed-square form:
after adding finite labels, prove Peiffer completeness uniformly in arity.

## Countercertificate

A counterexample must now produce more than a surviving Brunnian pure braid.
It should produce, for arbitrarily large `I`, an element

```text
a_I in K
```

and an invariant

```text
chi_I: K -> Q_I
```

such that `chi_I` kills the edge-degenerate subgroup, the Peiffer image
`im kappa`, and the fixed-base pullback subgroup
`Pi^sharp V_{p,q,B}^{I,Br}`, but

```text
chi_I(a_I) != 1.
```

Such a family proves that

```text
H_2^square(I;p,q|B) != 1
```

and hence

```text
R_{p,q}^I != 1.
```

This gives the precise negative route: high-arity crossed-square homology
surviving every fixed finite operator-label base.
