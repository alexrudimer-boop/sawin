# Point-Pushing Cyclic p-Power Tail

Date: 2026-05-30

This note refines the cyclic case isolated in
`proofs/point_pushing_central_monolith_depth.md`.

It does not prove outcome A or B.  It records the exact p-adic shape of a
cyclic central monolith obstruction and separates prime escape from p-power
depth escape.

## Setup

Let a product-prefix first failure have been compressed to a smallest
separating quotient

```text
phi:P_k(X)->H,
H ~= C_{p^e},
m = phi(w(h_1,...,h_k)) in M \ {1},
```

where `M` is the unique subgroup of order `p`.  Let `b(j)` be the product
prefix order bound: every finite group of order at most `b(j)` occurs among
the factors of `Pi_j`.

The monolithic-compression theorem gives

```text
|H| = p^e > b(j)
```

for every genuine product-prefix failure row.

## Theorem

Every cyclic p-power product-prefix failure is one of the following.

1. **Prime escape.**

   ```text
   p > b(j).
   ```

   Even the bottom prime group `C_p` is beyond the current product prefix.

2. **p-power depth escape.**

   ```text
   p <= b(j) < p^e.
   ```

   The prime `C_p` is already within the product prefix, but the moved element
   lies in the bottom subgroup of a deeper cyclic group `C_{p^e}`.  Every
   proper quotient of `C_{p^e}` kills that bottom element, so detecting the row
   requires the full p-power depth, not merely the prime.

Consequently, any infinite cyclic p-power B tail has an infinite subsequence
of one of these two forms:

```text
p_j -> infinity,
```

or

```text
p_j = p fixed and e_j -> infinity.
```

## Proof

The first dichotomy is immediate from `p^e>b(j)`.  Either `p>b(j)`, or
`p<=b(j)` and the strict inequality `p^e>b(j)` says the required p-power depth
is larger than the prefix.

For the proper-quotient statement, write `C_{p^e}` additively.  Its unique
order-`p` subgroup is generated by

```text
p^{e-1}.
```

Every proper quotient of `C_{p^e}` factors through `C_{p^d}` for some `d<e`.
The image of `p^{e-1}` in `C_{p^d}` is zero.  Thus no proper quotient
separates a nontrivial element of the monolith.  This is exactly why a
smallest separating quotient in the cyclic case is allowed to have large
p-power depth even when `C_p` itself is already present in the product prefix.

Finally, in any infinite sequence of cyclic p-power failures, either the
primes `p_j` are unbounded, giving a prime-escape subsequence, or some prime
`p` occurs infinitely often.  Along that fixed-prime subsequence, the orders
`p^{e_j}` must be unbounded because `b(j)->infinity` and `p^{e_j}>b(j)`.
Hence `e_j->infinity`.  QED.

## Consequence For B

The cyclic p-power branch of a negative proof is now exact.  It must produce
either:

```text
unbounded prime escape in cyclic bottom layers,
```

or

```text
fixed-prime unbounded p-adic depth.
```

The second case is stronger than a bounded cyclic quotient mover: it requires
the action image to keep a nontrivial element in the bottom subgroup of cyclic
quotients of arbitrarily large p-power order.

A positive proof can close the cyclic branch by proving a uniform bound on
these two phenomena for finite YBE point-pushing action images.
