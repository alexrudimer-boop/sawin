# Pure-braid law embedding

Date: 2026-05-28

This note records a more plausible normalized-law framework than pure powers.

## Construction

For `1 <= i < j`, let `A_{i,j}` be the standard pure braid

```text
A_{i,j} = sigma_{j-1} ... sigma_{i+1} sigma_i^2
          sigma_{i+1}^{-1} ... sigma_{j-1}^{-1}.
```

Given a free word `w(x_1,...,x_k)`, embed it in `P_{k+1}` by sending

```text
x_r -> A_{r,k+1}.
```

The code implements this as `law_word_on_last_strand(w, arity=k)`.

## Law-longitude lemma

If `w` is a law in a finite group `G`, then the braid

```text
beta_w = w(A_{1,k+1},...,A_{k,k+1})
```

has identity finite-`G` Artin-longitude signature:

```text
Lambda_{G,k+1}(beta_w)=Lambda_{G,k+1}(1).
```

This is an all-word statement, not a bounded search assertion.  The kernel of
the deletion map `P_{k+1} -> P_k` is the free point-pushing subgroup generated
by `A_{1,k+1},...,A_{k,k+1}`.  Under the Artin representation this subgroup
is the standard action of the free group on `k` puncture meridians obtained by
pushing the last puncture around a loop.  After assigning the free meridians
to elements of `G`, the pushed loop labelled by `w` evaluates to `1` because
`w` is a law in `G`.  Therefore the last puncture has trivial point-pushing
monodromy, and the induced conjugating longitudes of all puncture meridians
evaluate to the identity.  Equivalently, each recursive longitude of
`beta_w` lies in the verbal subgroup generated by substitution instances of
`w`; every homomorphism from the free group to `G` kills that verbal subgroup.

Thus pure-braid law words give normalized finite-group longitude invisibility
without needing the word to be a pure power.  This is stronger than the failed
pure-power shortcut because non-power law words, such as commutator-style
laws, need not be powers of one fixed braid action element.

The test suite verifies the executable convention:

- `A_{1,2} = sigma_1^2`;
- `A_{1,3} = sigma_2 sigma_1^2 sigma_2^{-1}`;
- the embedded commutator law is invisible to abelian test groups and visible
  to `S3`;
- embedded law braids are pure at the Artin permutation level.
- sampled words that are laws on `C2`, `C3`, and `S3` have identity
  finite-group longitude signatures on those groups.

The compact helper

```text
law_braid_longitude_subgroup_profile(groups,w,arity)
```

records the same invisibility condition through the subgroup
`V_beta(G)` generated by all recursive-longitude values.  For a law word on
`G`, this subgroup is trivial and the Artin permutation is identity.  This is
recorded separately in `proofs/longitude_subgroup_profile.md`.

## Remaining obstacle

This is not yet counterexample B.  To get B, one would need:

1. a fixed finite YBE solution `X`;
2. a sequence of free-group law words `w_j` that eventually hold in every
   finite group;
3. braid words `beta_j = w_j(A_{1,q_j},...,A_{k_j,q_j})` with `q_j -> infinity`;
4. the law-longitude lemma above, which proves that all recursive finite-G
   longitudes of `beta_j` are eventually trivial for every finite group `G`;
5. explicit tuples in `X^{q_j}` moved by `beta_j`.

The current framework supplies items 2-4 as a plausible route once an
appropriate finite YBE action is found.  It does not supply item 1 or item 5.
