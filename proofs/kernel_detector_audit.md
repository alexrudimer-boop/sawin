# Kernel detector audit

Date: 2026-05-28

This note records the first detector-candidate test after the kernel-block
Green lemma.

The kernel-block lemma says that a retained Green edge `(s,x)` induces a
permutation on the common kernel-block quotient of its regular Green
`R`-class.  There are two natural finite groups one might attach to this
data:

- the actual subgroup generated by the retained labels' induced permutations;
- the full symmetric group on the same kernel-block quotient.

The actual subgroup is too small in known finite-G-measurable branches.  The
first size-3 affine commutator candidate has actual kernel group `C_3`; the
commutator law braid is invisible to `C_3` and moves

```text
(0,0,1) -> (1,1,2).
```

The full symmetric group on the same three kernel blocks is `S_3`, and it sees
the commutator longitude.  Thus the detector candidate for the master theorem
cannot be only the actual kernel-action image.  It must include a sufficiently
large finite group, at least the full symmetric group on the relevant
kernel-block quotient, plus the already-bookkept known-branch detectors.

## Consequence for the master gap

This audit rules out a tempting but false shortcut: "use the actual
kernel-action image group."  That group misses ordinary affine commutator
motion.  The viable A-route group candidate must instead include the full
symmetric groups on finite kernel-block quotients, or another finite group at
least as law-sensitive for the relevant quotient actions.

This is still not a proof of the Master Local-Minimal Residual Theorem: the
bounded scan is finite evidence only.  Its value is that the bounded
counterexample pressure now points specifically at escaping the full symmetric
kernel-block groups, not merely escaping the actual kernel-action images.  The
remaining all-`n` problem is to prove that finite-G Artin-longitude data in
these symmetric kernel-block groups, together with the known-branch detector
groups, forces equality of the full residual braid action.  The new collision
scan is the bounded analogue of that full implication, while the blind-mover
scan is only the kernel form.

## Exact finite-image diagnostic

The code now also has an exact finite-image closure audit for a fixed braid
index `n`.  It generates the finite joint image of the braid group in:

- the base action;
- the symmetric kernel-block finite-group detector signatures;
- the residual total action.

When this audit is not truncated and has no kernel or collision failure, it is
an exact fixed-`n` proof of the sharp detector implication for that finite
joint image.  This is stronger than bounded word scans, but still not an
all-`n` theorem.

For the size-3 affine commutator candidate, the exact image closes at `n=2`.
The closed image has:

- `12` visited joint states;
- `12` detector states;
- `3` residual action states;
- `12` base-kernel detector states;
- `3` base-kernel residual states;
- no kernel failure and no detector/residual collision.

Thus the symmetric kernel-block detector proves the fixed-`n=2` implication
exactly for this diagnostic.  At `n=3`, even with integer-coded detector
states, the image grows past a diagnostic cap of `1000` states.  Under that
cap the audit sees `1001` detector states and `24` residual states without a
failure, but the entry is marked truncated and is not used as evidence for the
theorem.  Its purpose is to show that exact finite-image closure is feasible
at small braid index but becomes a state-growth problem at the first
nonabelian braid index.
