# YBE coskeletal mechanism

Date: 2026-06-03

The previous checkpoint separated fixed-base compactness from a bounded
pullback-coskeletal cutoff.  The sharper YBE-specific conclusion is now:
finite bijective YBE locality does not itself force such a cutoff.  The YBE
equation gives a local braid action, but it does not by itself make the
deletion coefficient tower have bounded cross-effects.

Let `C_bullet` be the marked point-pushing deletion tower of a finite
bijective set-theoretic solution, and let `A_bullet` be the coefficient
system of vertical fibre-bisection groups.  Fix a finite operator-label base
`B_bullet` with coefficient system `A_{B,bullet}` and comparison maps
`(Pi,iota)`.  Assume the Peiffer layer and cube transport defects have been
killed, so the remaining obstruction is a strict nonabelian deletion
`2`-cocycle

```text
Omega in Z^2_del(C;A)
```

modulo `1`-cochain gauge.

## Conditional theorem

A finite operator-label enrichment is `w`-local if every labelled
point-pushing groupoid is recovered from bounded restrictions:

```text
C_I ~= lim_{J subset I, |J| <= w} C_J.
```

The coefficient system must satisfy the analogous condition for deletion
sets `D` of size at most `3`:

```text
A_D^I ~= lim_{D subset J subset I, |J| <= |D| + w} A_D^J.
```

The base coefficient system must satisfy the same condition, and the
comparison maps

```text
iota_D^I: A_{B,D}^I -> A_D^I
```

must be the inverse-limit extensions of their bounded-subset restrictions.
Finally, the transport, cocycle, and gauge equations must be generated in
bounded relation arity `r`.  Since the strict deletion `2`-cocycle identity is
a triple-deletion identity, the natural cutoff is

```text
N0 = max(r, w + 3).
```

Under these hypotheses,

```text
[Omega] in im Pi^sharp
iff
[Omega_<=N0] in im Pi^sharp_<=N0.
```

Equivalently, all-arity base cocycle and all-arity gauge data satisfying

```text
u * Omega = Pi^sharp Theta
```

exist if and only if such data exist through arity `N0`.

The proof is formal once the `w`-locality hypotheses are available.
Nonabelian `1`-cochains, `2`-cochains, cocycles, gauges, and pullback
equations are built from finite products, finite fibre products, and finite
equalizers of the coefficient bands and transport maps.  Right Kan extension
from bounded arities is preserved by those finite-limit constructions.  The
cocycle equation uses at most three deleted strands, so the solution groupoid
is reconstructed from arity at most `w+3`, or from `r` if the chosen
operator-label presentation has larger relation arity.

Thus the real missing theorem is not "finite `X` implies finite cutoff."  It
is:

```text
after adding suitable finite operator labels, (C_bullet,A_bullet)
becomes w-local.
```

## Proof routes

The Fadell-Neuwirth route would prove `w`-locality recursively.  Forgetting
one strand gives a point-pushing extension whose vertical kernel is generated
by the new strand moving around the surviving labelled configuration.  A
bounded theorem would follow if every such vertical kernel were generated by
interactions with at most `w` surviving strands, and if all relations among
those generators were already visible with at most `w+1` or `w+2` survivors.

The Garside or finite-automaton route would choose normal forms for labelled
braid operators and show that stationarity, verticality, deletion, and
equality of fibre-bisections are recognized by a bounded-width synchronous
automaton.  Finite recognizability along each arity is not enough: the
transition congruence must be determined by bounded labelled
subconfigurations.

The FI/FB route would show that the coefficient bands `A_i^I`, `A_ij^I`,
and `A_ijk^I` are generated and related in bounded arity, with no higher
reduced cross-effects.  A useful target is: generated in degree `g`, related
in degree `r`, and no higher reduced cross-effects above degree `h`, giving

```text
N0 = max(g + 3, r, h + 3).
```

Finite generation alone is not enough for nonabelian orbit sets; compatible
low-arity data can still have hidden high-arity extension obstructions.

## Brunnian obstruction

For a two-deletion coefficient `A_ij^I`, define the reduced deletion
cross-effect

```text
cr_ij^I(A) =
intersection_{k in I \ {i,j}} ker(A_ij^I -> A_ij^{I \ {k}}).
```

These are vertical two-deletion bisections invisible after deleting any
additional surviving strand.

At the cocycle level, the relative Brunnian obstruction set is the quotient
of Brunnian deletion `2`-cocycles by Brunnian gauge coboundaries and
Brunnian pullbacks from the fixed base:

```text
Br^2_I(C,A;B)
= Z^2_Br,I(C;A) /
  ((C^1_Br,I * 1) . Pi^sharp Z^2_Br,I(B;A_B)).
```

If `Br^2_I(C,A;B)` is nontrivial for arbitrarily large `|I|`, then no
bounded pullback-coskeletal theorem can hold for that labelled tower.  For
any proposed cutoff `N0`, choose `|I| > N0`; the class restricts trivially to
every proper lower face, so it is invisible below arity `|I|`, but it is not
removed by gauge and is not pulled back from the fixed base.

Therefore bounded pullback-coskeletality for a fixed labelled tower is
equivalent to effective low-arity coefficient descent plus eventual
vanishing of these Brunnian relative deletion `2`-obstruction sets.
