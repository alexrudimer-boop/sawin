# Moving-variety counterexample criterion

Date: 2026-05-28

This note records a sharper B-route criterion for normalized-law
counterexamples.  It is not a counterexample; it is a way to certify that a
moving family of finite braid-action images really defeats every fixed finite
group detector.

## Finite detector laws

For a finite list of finite groups

```text
G_1, ..., G_r,
```

let `Law(G_1,...,G_r)` be the set of free words that evaluate to the identity
under every assignment into every `G_i`.  If a braid `beta` is obtained from
the standard pure-braid law embedding recorded in
`proofs/pure_braid_law_embedding.md`, the law-longitude lemma there proves
that

```text
Lambda_{G_i,n}(beta)=Lambda_{G_i,n}(1)
```

for every listed group `G_i`.

Thus, to defeat every fixed finite group it is enough to construct a sequence
`w_j` such that `w_j` is a law for every finite group of size at most `j`.
Every fixed finite group appears in this finite list once `j` is large enough.
Equivalently, after embedding `w_j` as a pure braid `beta_j`, the compact
longitude-subgroup profile must have trivial subgroup `V_beta_j(G)` for every
group in that finite list.  The helper
`law_braid_longitude_subgroup_profile()` records this finite invisibility
condition.

## Moving action-image obstruction

Let `X` be a candidate finite YBE solution.  For each `j`, choose a braid
index `q_j` and pure braid generators

```text
b_{j,1}, ..., b_{j,k_j} in P_{q_j}.
```

Let

```text
H_j = < rho_{X,q_j}(b_{j,1}), ..., rho_{X,q_j}(b_{j,k_j}) >
       <= Sym(X^{q_j}).
```

If there is a free word `w_j` such that

1. `w_j` is a law for every finite group of size at most `j`, and
2. `w_j` is not a law on `H_j`,

then the embedded braid

```text
beta_j = w_j(b_{j,1},...,b_{j,k_j})
```

has finite-G longitude data eventually trivial for every fixed finite group
`G`, but moves `X^{q_j}`.  The moved tuple is any tuple moved by the
nonidentity permutation `w_j(rho(b_{j,1}),...,rho(b_{j,k_j}))`.

This supplies the normalized-law obstruction sequence required for outcome B,
provided the braid generators are the standard last-strand pure generators
`A_{r,q_j}` or another embedding with the same all-finite-group law
implication.

## Why this is stronger than pure powers

The failed pure-power route chooses

```text
w_j = x^{lcm(1,...,j)}.
```

That only tests whether the moving action images have element orders with
new prime-power divisors.  The moving-variety criterion allows arbitrary
non-power laws.  A candidate can therefore escape by having pure action
groups `H_j` that do not belong to the finite group variety generated by all
groups of size at most `j`, even if every individual pure generator has
bounded order.

## Executable diagnostic

The helper

```text
short_law_separating_groups(law_groups, target_group, arity, max_length)
```

searches reduced free words and returns a word that is a law on every group
in `law_groups` but not a law on `target_group`.  The test suite verifies the
basic separation

```text
C_2, C_3  versus  S_3
```

by finding a commutator-type law: it holds on the abelian cyclic groups and
fails on `S_3`.

This diagnostic is finite search only.  It can support a B proof only after a
symbolic construction supplies, for every `j`, the finite law group list, the
moving YBE action image `H_j`, a separating word `w_j`, and a proof that the
corresponding embedded braid has the required Artin-longitude law property.
The companion helpers `longitude_subgroup_mover_profiles()` and
`residual_longitude_subgroup_mover_profiles()` attach a concrete moved tuple
to the finite longitude-subgroup profile, so a proposed sequence can be
audited for both sides of B: finite-group invisibility and nontrivial YBE
motion.  These helpers are still bounded diagnostics, not a substitute for
the symbolic normalized-law sequence.

For the pure-braid embedding, it is often enough to test a specific generator
assignment rather than law failure on a whole target group.  The structure
orbit helper `structure_orbit_law_separation()` therefore searches for a word
that is a law on the supplied finite detector groups but evaluates
nontrivially on the assigned tuple of restricted pure-braid generators
`(A_{1,q}|_O,...,A_{q-1,q}|_O)`.  This is the exact finite condition needed
to produce a moved tuple from the embedded braid, while the orbit group size
is retained only as context when it can be closed.
The assignment-specific core is now exposed directly as
`short_law_separating_permutation_assignment(...)`; see
`proofs/assigned_generator_law_separation.md`.  This avoids conflating
"not a law on the generated target group" with the sharper condition
"nontrivial on the actual assigned pure-generator tuple."

## Current impact on the main problem

The criterion makes the remaining B-route precise:

- find a fixed finite YBE solution whose pure braid action images have
  unbounded finite-variety complexity in the sense above; or
- prove that every finite local-minimal interval fails this moving-variety
  escape because its residual action factors through one fixed finite group
  of longitude evaluations.

The structure-orbit refinement in
`proofs/structure_orbit_law_obstruction.md` strengthens the first bullet:
the separating word can be required to move a tuple inside one degree
structure orbit.  This removes false positives caused only by growth of
global product/orbit invariants and matches the Green/corridor holonomy
target.

The second alternative is exactly the A-route finite-longitude factorization
program.  Therefore this criterion does not change the final theorem burden,
but it prevents a false counterexample proof based only on growing finite
images or bounded non-power law searches.

The contrapositive guardrail is recorded in
`proofs/fixed_variety_barrier.md`: if all moving action or holonomy groups
in the proposed route lie in the variety generated by one fixed finite group
`G_0`, then every normalized-law sequence eventually invisible to `G_0`
acts trivially on those groups.  Thus a B proof must show finite-variety
escape from every fixed `G_0`.
