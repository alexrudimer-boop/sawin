# Point-Pushing Monolith Type Split

Date: 2026-05-30

This note records the finite-group structure forced by
`proofs/point_pushing_monolithic_compression.md`.  Once a product-prefix
failure is compressed to a smallest separating quotient, the remaining
obstruction has only two possible monolith types.

It does not prove outcome A or B.  It splits the exact B target into an
abelian affine-type tail and a nonabelian characteristically-simple tail.

## Setup

Let a product-prefix first-failure row be compressed to

```text
phi:P_k(X)->H,
M normal H,
phi(w(h_1,...,h_k)) in M \ {1},
```

where `H` is finite monolithic and `M` is its unique minimal nontrivial normal
subgroup.

## Theorem

The monolith `M` is characteristically simple.  Hence exactly one of the
following holds.

1. **Abelian monolith.**  There is a prime `p` and an integer `r>=1` such that

   ```text
   M ~= (C_p)^r.
   ```

2. **Nonabelian monolith.**  There is a nonabelian finite simple group `S` and
   an integer `r>=1` such that

   ```text
   M ~= S^r.
   ```

Moreover, in the nonabelian case the conjugation action of `H` permutes the
`r` simple direct factors transitively.

## Proof

Let `C` be a characteristic subgroup of `M`.  Since `M normal H` and `C` is
characteristic in `M`, the subgroup `C` is normal in `H`.  By minimality of
`M`, either

```text
C=1
```

or

```text
C=M.
```

Thus `M` is characteristically simple.

A finite characteristically simple group is a direct product of isomorphic
finite simple groups.  Indeed, choose a minimal nontrivial normal subgroup
`S` of `M`.  The subgroup generated by all automorphic images of `S` is
characteristic and nontrivial, hence it is all of `M`.  These automorphic
images are minimal normal subgroups of `M`; distinct minimal normal subgroups
intersect trivially and commute with one another.  Therefore `M` is the direct
product of isomorphic finite simple factors.

If the simple factor is abelian, it is cyclic of prime order `C_p`, so

```text
M ~= (C_p)^r.
```

If the simple factor is nonabelian, then

```text
M ~= S^r
```

for a nonabelian finite simple group `S`.

Finally, the factors in the direct product form a characteristic block system
for `M`, and conjugation by `H` permutes them.  If the action on factors had
more than one orbit, the product of the factors in one orbit would be a
proper nontrivial normal subgroup of `H`, contradicting minimality of `M`.
Thus the action is transitive.  QED.

## Consequence For B

After passing to an infinite subsequence, any product-prefix monolithic
failure tail is one of two broad forms:

```text
abelian monolith tail,
nonabelian monolith tail.
```

In the abelian tail, the moved value survives in an elementary abelian
monolith `(C_p)^r`; a positive proof may try to kill it by a uniform linear or
affine detector argument.

In the nonabelian tail, the moved value survives in a transitive product
`S^r` of nonabelian finite simple groups; a positive proof may try to rule out
unbounded nonabelian composition-factor tails in the point-pushing action
images.

Thus a final B proof cannot present an arbitrary quotient mover.  It must
produce an unbounded sequence in one of these two monolith regimes.

## Audit Hook

The helper

```text
point_pushing_monolithic_compression_audit(...)
```

now records:

```text
monolith_type,
monolith_prime,
monolith_element_orders.
```

For small finite rows this confirms whether the compressed monolith is
`elementary_abelian` or `nonabelian_characteristically_simple`.  The helper is
only a finite certificate check; the theorem above supplies the symbolic
classification.
