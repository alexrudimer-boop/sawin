# Universal symmetric detector target

Date: 2026-05-28

This note records a possible direct route to Sawin finite-rack domination that
is stronger than the local-minimal congruence-chain program.

## Candidate theorem

Let `X` be a finite bijective set-theoretic YBE solution and set

```text
G_X = Sym(X).
```

The candidate assertion is:

```text
Lambda_{G_X,n}(beta)=Lambda_{G_X,n}(1)
    => rho_{X,n}(beta)=1
```

for every braid index `n` and every braid `beta in B_n`.

If true, the sharp obstruction theorem applies with the one-point quotient
and the finite rack

```text
Y_X = A_{Sym(X)}.
```

Thus `Y_X` would dominate `X` for all braid indices.  The rack is explicit,
finite, and depends only on `X`, not on `n`.

## Why this target is plausible

Every finite rack action on `X` is detected by Artin longitudes in a subgroup
of `Sym(X)`, namely its inner group.  Many known finite-G-measurable YBE
branches also use detector groups that arise from finite permutation actions
on fibres, kernel blocks, Green classes, product-label blocks, or affine
coordinates.  The symmetric detector candidate asks whether the full
symmetric group on the underlying finite set is already law-sensitive enough
to dominate all of these finite permutation shadows simultaneously.

The generated audit `proofs/symmetric_detector_audit.md` supports the
candidate only diagnostically:

- exact finite-image closure proves the fixed-index implication for all
  size-`2` solutions at `n=2,3` and all size-`3` solutions at `n=2`;
- at size `3`, `n=3`, the identity-action row is now separated out as a
  trivial implication proof, while the other `72` rows still truncate in the
  detector-image closure with no failure observed before the cap;
- the symbolic filter in `proofs/direct_symmetric_known_branches.md` explains
  all size-`2` and size-`3` rows by branch proofs: rack inner group,
  involutive Artin permutation quotient, permutation-form twist subgroup, or
  nondegenerate guitar-derived rack.  Thus the exact `n=3` truncations in
  the tiny corpus are not direct-Sym obstruction candidates;
- bounded scans find no size-`2` or size-`3` failure at `n=3,4` through the
  recorded length bound;
- the structure-orbit symmetric-law separator audit finds no length-`<=6`
  pure-law mover against `Sym(X)` for all size-`2` solutions at `n=3,4`, all
  size-`3` solutions at `n=3`, or the standard size-three stress rows at
  `n=4`;
- affine cyclic two-strand scans over `Z/m`, `2<=m<=8`, show no crossing
  order obstruction.
- the executable helper `symmetric_detector_readout_audit(X,n)` now packages
  the one-point quotient and the single detector group `Sym(X)` into the
  fixed-index readout audit.  On the size-three affine stress row it proves
  the direct symmetric readout at `n=2` exactly, with `12` reachable
  base-kernel detector states and `3` residual action states.

None of this is theorem evidence for arbitrary fibres or arbitrary braid
index.  Its value is that the candidate has a clear symbolic form.

## Two-strand obstruction test

For `n=2`, the braid group is generated by one crossing `sigma_1`.  The first
nontrivial positive power invisible to `Sym(X)` is exactly

```text
sigma_1^(2*exp(Sym(X))) = sigma_1^(2*lcm(1,...,|X|)).
```

The exact proof is recorded in
`proofs/two_strand_symmetric_gate.md`: for any finite group `G`,
`Lambda_{G,2}(sigma_1^k)=Lambda_{G,2}(1)` if and only if
`2 exp(G)` divides `k`.  Therefore a two-strand counterexample to the
symmetric detector candidate
would be a finite YBE solution whose crossing permutation

```text
R_X : X^2 -> X^2
```

has order not dividing `2*lcm(1,...,|X|)`.  Such an example would not by
itself prove outcome B, because a larger finite group might still detect the
solution.  It would, however, kill the direct `A_{Sym(X)}` route.
The helper `two_strand_symmetric_detector_failure_certificate(X)` now returns
the explicit braid `sigma_1^(2*exp(Sym(X)))` and a moved tuple whenever this
divisibility fails.  Thus any future two-strand direct-symmetric failure is
immediately converted into an auditable braid-action certificate, while still
remaining short of a Sawin counterexample unless every finite group is
defeated.

The affine cyclic stress test found no such obstruction for the recorded
small moduli.

The generated audit now also records the exact crossing-order gate in the
exhaustive size-`2` and size-`3` corpora.  It finds no bad rows:

```text
size 2: period 4, order histogram {1:1, 2:2, 4:2}
size 3: period 12, order histogram {1:1, 2:18, 3:12, 4:36, 6:6}
```

This is finite evidence only.  The symbolic theorem still needed for the
direct `A_{Sym(X)}` route is the all-`n` factorization, or at least a proof
that the exact two-strand divisibility holds for arbitrary finite bijective
YBE solutions.

For nondegenerate solutions, the two-strand test is now closed by the
derived-rack/guitar branch recorded in `proofs/two_strand_guitar_gate.md`.
Writing `R(x,y)=(sigma_x(y),rho_y(x))`, the derived operation
`a op b = sigma_a(rho_{sigma_b^{-1}(a)}(b))` is a rack operation, and
`J_2(x,y)=(sigma_x(y),x)` conjugates the original crossing to the derived
rack crossing.  The rack inner group embeds in `Sym(X)`, so the crossing
order divides `2 exp(Sym(X))`.

This does not prove the arbitrary bijective theorem: degenerate bijective YBE
solutions need not have the nondegenerate guitar conjugacy.  The direct
`Sym(X)` target must therefore still prove a replacement in the degenerate
case, or exhibit a degenerate solution whose crossing order escapes the
two-strand bound.

The branch note `proofs/two_strand_known_branch_gate.md` proves the exact
two-strand gate for two simpler known branches.  Rack-type solutions pass
because their `B_2` action factors through `Inn(X) <= Sym(X)`.  Permutation
solutions `R(x,y)=(sigma(y),tau(x))` pass because
`R^2=(sigma tau) x (sigma tau)`, so `ord(R)=2 ord(sigma tau)` when
`|X|>1`.  Together with the derived-rack branch, the exhaustive size-`3`
crossing-gate audit now has no `passes_unclassified` rows.

The product note `proofs/two_strand_product_gate.md` adds another symbolic
closure property: if two finite solutions pass the direct symmetric
two-strand gate, then their Cartesian product passes it.  The product
crossing order is the least common multiple of the factor crossing orders,
and both factor symmetric periods divide the product symmetric period
`2*lcm(1,...,|X||Y|)`.  Therefore a first two-strand failure of the direct
`A_{Sym(X)}` route cannot be manufactured by taking products of smaller
passing solutions.

## Relation to the local theorem

The symmetric detector target is stronger than the Master Local-Minimal
Residual Theorem.  If it is proved for every whole finite YBE solution, the
congruence-chain induction is unnecessary for outcome A.

There is one important guardrail: this proof cannot use the literal rack
coordinate formula

```text
rho_X(beta)(x)_j = phi_x(L_j(beta))(x_{p(j)}).
```

For a single positive generator, `L_{i+1}=1` and `p(i+1)=i`, so such a formula
would force the second output of every crossing to be the left input.  That
is exactly the rack-type condition.  The direct `Sym(X)` route must therefore
prove a global factorization through the Artin action on `W_{Sym(X)}(n)`,
not a coordinatewise rack-action formula on `X`.

If the target fails, the failure is still useful.  A single braid invisible to
`Sym(X)` but moving `X^n` would identify a concrete residual mechanism not
controlled by the largest obvious permutation detector on `X`.  The next
step would then be to determine whether that mechanism is detected by some
other fixed finite group, or whether every finite group fails and the
diagonal normalized-obstruction lemma yields outcome B.

## Current symbolic gap

The missing proof is a factorization statement:

> For every finite bijective YBE solution `X`, the braid action on `X^n`
> factors through the Artin action on the relatively free quotient
> `W_{Sym(X)}(n)` for all `n`.

Equivalently, every coordinate change induced by a braid should be expressible
as a finite combination of recursive Artin-longitude evaluations in
`Sym(X)`.  This is known for racks and for several branch normal forms, but
it is not yet proved for arbitrary bijective YBE tables.

The helper `symmetric_detector_readout_audit(X,n)` gives the fixed-degree
shadow of this missing statement.  It can certify that the readout exists for
one `n`, but the required theorem is a uniform construction of that readout
from the fixed group `Sym(X)` for all braid indices.
