# Unit holonomy longitude gate

Date: 2026-05-29

This note records a reusable bridge between finite transition semigroups and
the finite-group Artin-longitude detector.  It does not prove the Master
Local-Minimal Residual Theorem by itself.  Its purpose is to make precise
which part of a finite semigroup observer can contribute to a genuine
residual braid motion.

## Setup

Let `M` be a finite transformation monoid on a finite set `S`.  Write

```text
U(M) = {m in M : m is a permutation of S}.
```

The set `U(M)` is a finite group under composition.  Indeed, it is closed
under composition, contains the identity, and if `u in U(M)` then some
positive power of `u` is the identity, so `u^{-1}` is also a positive power of
`u` and lies in `M`.

For a braid `beta in B_n`, let

```text
V_beta(U(M)) =
  < phi(L_i(beta)) : phi:F_n -> U(M), 1<=i<=n > <= U(M)
```

be the subgroup generated by all recursive Artin-longitude values in this
unit group.

## Gate Lemma

Suppose a residual branch has finite semigroup labels

```text
h_1(beta),...,h_r(beta) in M
```

and suppose the actual residual motion is a permutation whenever it is read in
the finite observer.  Then every label that can be responsible for nontrivial
residual motion must lie in `U(M)`.  Nonunit labels are reset-like or
information-losing observer artifacts; they cannot by themselves be the
permutation induced by a residual braid element.

If, moreover,

```text
h_j(beta) in V_beta(U(M))
```

for every unit label `h_j(beta)`, then the finite-group longitude identity
over the fixed group `U(M)` kills all these labels:

```text
Lambda_{U(M),n}(beta)=Lambda_{U(M),n}(1)
    => h_j(beta)=1 for all j.
```

Proof.  The longitude identity says that every recursive longitude evaluates
to the identity under every homomorphism `F_n -> U(M)`.  Hence every generator
of `V_beta(U(M))` is the identity, so `V_beta(U(M))` is the trivial subgroup.
Any label lying in it is therefore the identity.  Nonunit labels cannot be
the residual permutation part by the first paragraph; the aperiodic
permutation lemma in `proofs/finite_semigroup_holonomy_route.md` gives the
same conclusion for purely aperiodic transition components.

The separate unit-factorization gate
`proofs/unit_factorization_gate.md` removes one more ambiguity: if a residual
branch is represented as a product of finite total transformations and the
product is a permutation, then every factor in that product is already a unit.
Thus nonunit labels cannot be hidden inside a longer word whose final effect
is a genuine residual permutation.

## Consequence For A

In a Green/corridor or completed-context proof, it is enough to route every
nontrivial residual motion into the unit groups of the finite transition
monoids attached to the interval and then prove the subgroup membership

```text
h_j(beta) in V_beta(U(M)).
```

Those unit groups are fixed finite groups built from the interval data, not
from the braid index.  They can therefore be multiplied with the existing
Green kernel-block, Schutzenberger, product-label, quotient, and known-branch
detector factors.  The sharp obstruction theorem then supplies the rack
factor `A_G`.

This is still a factorization target, not a completed proof: the missing
master-local step is to prove the subgroup membership for all braid degrees,
base tuples, and residual input tuples in the remaining local-minimal
branches.

## Consequence For B

A counterexample cannot cite a nonunit reset label in a finite observer as the
moving obstruction.  To become outcome B, it must produce a normalized-law
sequence whose residual motion survives in the group part:

- a compatible sequence of unit labels or group-like corridor loops;
- nontrivial moved tuples in the actual residual braid action;
- eventual invisibility of the Artin longitudes in every finite group.

Equivalently, the B obstruction must escape every fixed finite unit-holonomy
group by normalized laws, not merely show that bounded observers have
non-bijective or aperiodic collapses.

## Executable Certificate

The helper

```text
unit_longitude_subgroup_audit(monoid,n,beta,labels)
```

extracts `U(M)` as `monoid_permutation_group(monoid)`, computes
`V_beta(U(M))`, and reports:

- the Artin permutation of `beta`;
- the order of the unit group;
- the size of the longitude-value subgroup;
- labels that are not units;
- unit labels outside the longitude-value subgroup.

The tests in `tests/test_semigroup_holonomy.py` record the convention:
for the order-two unit group generated by a swap, the braid `sigma_1^2` has a
full longitude-value subgroup and sees the swap, while `sigma_1^4` has
trivial longitude-value subgroup in that group and therefore does not see the
swap.

The functoriality note `proofs/longitude_subgroup_functoriality.md` applies
after the unit group is extracted.  If a proof quotients the unit group or
projects a detector product onto this unit factor, longitude-value subgroup
membership is preserved under that fixed finite group map.

The companion helper

```text
unit_factorization_audit(factors)
```

checks the prerequisite that a proposed residual word of transformations is
actually in the unit part.  It reports nonunit factors and whether the
composite is a permutation.
