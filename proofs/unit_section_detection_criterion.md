# Unit section detection criterion

## Purpose

This note upgrades the unit-factorization and unit-longitude gates into a
single reusable local detector criterion.  It does not prove the remaining
Green/corridor factorization theorem by itself; it records exactly what a
future all-`n` branch proof must supply once a finite transformation-monoid
observer has been fixed.

## Criterion

Let `M` be a finite transformation monoid on a finite set `S`, and let

```text
U(M) = {m in M : m is a permutation of S}
```

be its unit/permutation group.  Fix a braid word `beta in B_n`.  Suppose a
residual branch map is represented inside `M` by a factor word

```text
f_k ... f_1.
```

Assume:

1. the branch map is a permutation of `S`;
2. every factor `f_i` belongs to `M`;
3. every unit factor belongs to the Artin longitude-value subgroup
   `V_beta(U(M))`.

Then identity finite-`U(M)` Artin-longitude data for `beta` forces the branch
map to be the identity.

Proof.  Since the branch map is a permutation, the unit-factorization lemma
in `proofs/unit_factorization_gate.md` forces every factor `f_i` to be a
permutation, hence an element of `U(M)`.  By assumption each `f_i` lies in
`V_beta(U(M))`.  If the finite-`U(M)` longitude data of `beta` is identical
to that of the identity braid, then the Artin permutation is trivial and every
recursive longitude value in `U(M)` is the group identity.  Therefore
`V_beta(U(M))` is the trivial subgroup, so every `f_i` is the identity and
their product is the identity.

Thus the only missing input for this criterion in the universal corridor
branch is the all-`n` section factorization: every residual coordinate motion
must be written as such a word in one fixed finite monoid whose unit labels
are generated by recursive Artin-longitude values in the fixed unit group.

## A-Route Use

For a local-minimal interval `pi:X->Z`, the desired proof-side pattern is:

1. construct a finite transition monoid `M(pi,Q)` from the interval and the
   quotient detector, independent of `n`;
2. prove that every residual coordinate branch of every
   `beta in ker rho_{Q,n}` admits a factor word in `M(pi,Q)`;
3. prove that every unit section label in that word lies in
   `V_beta(U(M(pi,Q)))`.

Then the finite group `G(pi,Q)=U(M(pi,Q))`, possibly multiplied by the other
already closed detector factors, satisfies the sharp kernel implication.  The
sharp rack for the interval is `Q x A_G`.

This statement is deliberately input-dependent: the factor word may depend on
`n`, `beta`, the base tuple, the fibre tuple, and the output coordinate.  The
monoid `M(pi,Q)` and its unit group may not depend on `n`.

The endpoint criterion in `proofs/unit_composite_longitude_criterion.md`
weakens this sufficient condition.  It is enough to prove that the final
residual unit/composite belongs to `V_beta(U(M))`; individual section factors
may telescope and need not each lie in the subgroup.

## B-Route Consequence

A counterexample through a finite semigroup/corridor observer cannot use a
reset label as the moving obstruction.  If the final residual action is a
permutation, all labels in its successful factor word are already units.  A
B certificate must therefore produce a normalized-law sequence whose
nontrivial motion survives in these finite unit groups, and then prove that
the survival defeats every finite group detector.  A nonunit branch label is
only evidence that the proposed factorization is not yet a residual
permutation branch.

## Executable Audit

The helper

```text
unit_section_detection_audit(monoid,n,beta,factors)
```

combines:

- `unit_factorization_audit(factors)`;
- `unit_longitude_subgroup_audit(monoid,n,beta,factors)`;
- membership of all factors in the finite monoid.

It reports whether the supplied word is a permutation branch, whether its
factors are unit sections, whether they lie in `V_beta(U(M))`, and whether an
identity longitude signature kills the composite.  The tests include:

- a nontrivial unit branch whose label lies in `V_beta(C_2)`;
- an identity-signature branch where only the identity label survives;
- a reset branch, correctly rejected as a non-permutation residual branch.
