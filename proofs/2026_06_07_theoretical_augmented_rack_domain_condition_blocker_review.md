## Positive Blocker

group `G <= Sym(Omega)` generated by total extensions of the finite contextual
partial translations, and then define the augmented rack

```text

(omega,g) * (eta,h) = (g eta, g h g^(-1)).
```

This avoids explicitly choosing values for non-fillable products.  However, to
run the contextual induction across a forced compatible crossing, one needs:

```text

  =>  h k h^(-1) extends L_{p*q}.
```

For a point `r` in `dom(L_{p*q})`, this requires both domain conditions

```text
h^(-1)(r) in dom(L_q),
k h^(-1)(r) in dom(L_p).
```

These are precisely joint-fillability requirements.  They say that the point
`r` must arise from a realizable triple behind the crossing, not merely that
`p*q` and `r` are separately compatible.

The Yang-Baxter equation proves the identity on jointly realizable triples.
It does not force these domain conditions for virtual or non-fillable
contextual states.  Arbitrary finite total extensions of the partial
translations can therefore break the next crossing.

Constraining the total extensions to satisfy all such identities is exactly
the unresolved finite totalization/separability problem for the contextual
Wirtinger data.
