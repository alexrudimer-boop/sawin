# Product holonomy normalization

Date: 2026-05-28

This note records a sharper finite target for the genuinely coloured product
branch.  It is not the missing all-`n` product theorem; it identifies the
finite holonomy that actually remains after the coboundary part of a product
label word is gauged away.

## Gauge-normalized labels

For a swapped or direct product interval, the coboundary audit chooses fibre
gauges

```text
g_a : A_a -> B_C
```

on each connected component of the product-label colour graph.  For a
composable product-label word

```text
f : A_s -> A_t
```

define its normalized holonomy label by

```text
N(f) = g_t f g_s^{-1} in Sym(B_C).
```

If the product labels are a coboundary, then every normalized label is the
identity.  If the coboundary audit fails, the nonidentity normalized labels
generate the finite product holonomy groups already reported by
`swapped_product_holonomy_summary()` and
`direct_product_holonomy_summary()`.

The executable helpers are:

```text
product_holonomy_label_permutation(interval, branch, source_color, label_word)
product_holonomy_closed_label_permutations(interval, branch, z, beta)
product_holonomy_groups(interval, branch)
```

The first evaluates one formal product-label word as `g_t f g_s^{-1}`.  The
second applies this to all closed product labels in the sharp-kernel setting:
the base colour tuple is fixed and the Artin permutation is trivial, so every
coordinate label is closed.  The third returns the finite permutation groups
generated by the normalized holonomy labels on each model fibre.

## Longitude-subgroup audit

The normalized analogue of the product-label subgroup criterion is:

```text
product_holonomy_longitude_subgroup_audit(interval, branch, z, beta)
```

For every nonidentity normalized closed label, this helper:

1. selects the finite holonomy group attached to its model fibre;
2. evaluates every recursive Artin longitude of `beta` under every assignment
   `F_n -> H`;
3. closes the subgroup generated by those longitude values;
4. checks whether the normalized closed label lies in that subgroup.

The companion

```text
product_holonomy_longitude_subgroup_failures(...)
```

searches a supplied finite word list for violations of this normalized
criterion.  Like all bounded scanners here, it is not a theorem; it is a way
to target the exact all-`n` statement or find a sharper candidate for outcome
B.

## Relation to the product detector target

The earlier product target used the totalized tagged-fibre label group
`H_prod`.  That group is canonical and finite, but it contains colour-transport
bookkeeping that vanishes by gauge telescoping on coboundary paths.  The
normalized holonomy formulation removes this bookkeeping:

> Product holonomy-longitude target.  For every product local-minimal
> interval, every braid degree `n`, closed base tuple `z`, residual braid
> `beta`, and coordinate `j`, the normalized closed label
> `N(h_{z,j}(beta))` lies in the subgroup generated by recursive Artin
> longitude values in the fixed finite product holonomy group, possibly
> multiplied by the already known cyclic, affine, coboundary, and
> whole-branch detector factors.

This target is equivalent to the product-label target after gauges are chosen:
the coboundary part telescopes to `g_{z_j}^{-1}g_{z_j}=1` on closed paths, and
only the normalized holonomy part can move the fibre.  If the normalized label
lies in the longitude-value subgroup, identity finite-G longitude data kills
it.

## Guardrails

The normalized holonomy group is still not a proof by itself.  A finite
nontrivial holonomy generator is only a possible detector factor; a proof must
show that all braid-produced closed holonomy labels are products of recursive
longitude evaluations in the fixed group, uniformly in braid degree.

Two regression tests lock the conventions:

- coboundary product labels normalize to the identity;
- the one-colour commuting swapped branch normalizes `sigma_1^2` to the
  expected cyclic pairwise-linking permutation, and its normalized holonomy
  lies in the corresponding `C_3` longitude-value subgroup.

The generated audit `proofs/product_holonomy_subgroup_audit.md` applies the
same normalized subgroup criterion to the smallest arbitrary product corpus:
two quotient colours, two-point fibres, and every two-point quotient YBE
base.  It scans `92` local-minimal product branches and finds `2,048`
nonidentity normalized holonomy rows with `0` subgroup failures.  This is
finite candidate evidence only; it is not a substitute for the all-`n`
product holonomy-longitude theorem.

The generated audit `proofs/product_holonomy_exact_audit.md` adds a
fixed-degree image-closure guardrail on representative tuples.  It closes the
whole finite braid image for those tuples, so there is no word-length cutoff
inside the displayed checks.  The exact rows show that raw normalized
holonomy alone is not the final product detector: it misses a fixed-degree
closed label in a row whose total interval is already in a known
nondegenerate/permutation branch.  Multiplying by the symmetric group on the
four total points removes that displayed miss.  Therefore the theorem target
must keep the detector-product form: normalized holonomy factors together
with the already-known cyclic, affine, coboundary, or whole-branch detector
factors.
