# Point-pushing fixed-variety domination criterion

Date: 2026-05-30

This note follows `proofs/sawin_last_strand_law_reduction.md` and
`proofs/point_pushing_action_image_variety.md`.  It does not prove outcome A
or B.  It records the exact global reformulation obtained by allowing the
fixed detector to be an arbitrary finite group, not only a symmetric group.

## Setup

Let `X` be a finite bijective set-theoretic YBE solution.  For `k>=1`, set
`n=k+1` and define the marked point-pushing action image

```text
P_k(X) =
< rho_{X,n}(A_{1,n}), ..., rho_{X,n}(A_{k,n}) >
<= Sym(X^n).
```

For a finite group `G`, let `var(G)` denote the group variety generated by
`G`, equivalently the finite groups satisfying every word law of `G`.

## Theorem

For a fixed finite group `G`, the following are equivalent.

1. The sharp detector rack `A_G` dominates `X`:

   ```text
   K_G(n) subset ker rho_{X,n}
   ```

   for every braid index `n`.

2. Every point-pushing action image lies in the fixed variety generated by
   `G`:

   ```text
   P_k(X) in var(G)
   ```

   for every `k>=1`.

Consequently, Sawin finite-rack domination for `X` is equivalent to:

```text
there exists one finite group G such that P_k(X) in var(G) for all k>=1.
```

## Proof

Assume (1).  Fix `k` and let `w in F_k` be any law of `G`.  By the
last-strand point-pushing exactness lemma,

```text
iota_{k+1}(w) in K_G(k+1).
```

Since `K_G(k+1) subset ker rho_{X,k+1}`, the action of
`iota_{k+1}(w)` on `X^{k+1}` is trivial.  But this action is exactly

```text
w(rho(A_{1,k+1}), ..., rho(A_{k,k+1})).
```

Thus every law of `G` holds on the marked generators of `P_k(X)`.  Since the
marked generators generate `P_k(X)`, every law of `G` holds on all of
`P_k(X)`: substituting words in the marked generators for arbitrary elements
of `P_k(X)` reduces each evaluation to the point-pushing case.  Therefore
`P_k(X) in var(G)`.

Conversely assume (2).  Suppose

```text
beta in K_G(n)
```

and `rho_{X,n}(beta) != 1`.  By the moving-layer extraction lemma in
`proofs/sawin_last_strand_law_reduction.md`, with the right-stabilized
Fadell-Neuwirth convention made explicit in
`proofs/fadell_neuwirth_layer_extraction_guardrail.md`, there are `r<=n` and

```text
w in Law_{r-1}(G)
```

such that

```text
rho_{X,r}(iota_r(w)) != 1.
```

But `P_{r-1}(X) in var(G)` by (2), so every law of `G` evaluates trivially on
the marked point-pushing action image.  Hence `rho_{X,r}(iota_r(w))=1`, a
contradiction.  Therefore no such moving `beta` exists, and
`K_G(n) subset ker rho_{X,n}` for all `n`.

The final statement follows from the sharp obstruction theorem: if such `G`
exists, the finite rack `A_G` dominates `X`; if a finite rack dominates `X`,
then the normalized-law domination dichotomy supplies such a finite group
detector `G`.

QED.

## Consequence

The global problem is now equivalent to a pure fixed-variety containment
statement for the sequence of point-pushing images:

```text
P_1(X), P_2(X), P_3(X), ...
```

Positive route:

```text
construct one finite group G_X such that P_k(X) in var(G_X) for all k.
```

Negative route:

```text
for every finite group G, find k and a law of G that fails on P_k(X),
```

and organize those failures into the symmetric or product-prefix
normalized-law sequence already recorded in the repository.

This formulation is weaker than constructing explicit endpoint-longitude
expressions and is not by itself a local proof.  It is, however, exactly
equivalent to the global finite-rack domination question.
