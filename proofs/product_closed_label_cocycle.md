# Product closed-label cocycle target

Date: 2026-05-28

This note sharpens the genuinely coloured product-permutation branch.  It is
not a proof of the Master Local-Minimal Residual Theorem.  Its purpose is to
separate the theorem-level product obligation from two tempting but invalid
shortcuts.

## Labelled braid groupoid

Let `pi : X -> Z` be a local product interval.  In the swapped branch,

```text
T_{a,b}(x,y) = (L_{a,b}(y), R_{a,b}(x)),
```

and in the direct branch,

```text
T_{a,b}(x,y) = (L_{a,b}(x), R_{a,b}(y)).
```

For a base colour tuple `z in Z^n`, the helpers

```text
swapped_product_label_word_action(interval,z,beta)
direct_product_label_word_action(interval,z,beta)
```

lift a braid word to a labelled path in the finite colour groupoid whose
arrows are the fibre bijections `L_{a,b}^{+/-1}` and `R_{a,b}^{+/-1}`.
Evaluating those formal words gives the residual coordinate maps exactly.

When `beta` lies in the sharp-kernel setting, the base tuple is fixed by the
dominating rack `Q`.  If the finite-group longitude signature is also
identity, then the Artin permutation is trivial.  Therefore, in the swapped
branch the dependency vector is identity, and in the direct branch it was
identity already.  Each residual coordinate is then a closed label

```text
h_{z,j}(beta) in Aut(A_{z_j})
```

or, after the totalization in `proofs/product_label_group_detector.md`, an
element of the fixed finite group `H_prod`.

The helper

```text
product_closed_label_permutations(interval,branch,z,beta)
```

is the executable version of this object.  It requires the colour tuple to be
closed, and in the swapped branch it also requires the dependency vector to be
identity.  It returns the totalized permutations representing the closed
labels `h_{z,j}(beta)`.

Thus the product branch is closed exactly when these closed labels are forced
to be identity by finite-group Artin-longitude data.

The strongest current finite fixed-word formulation is the
longitude-subgroup version recorded in
`proofs/product_longitude_subgroup_criterion.md`.  For the fixed totalized
label group `H_prod`, form the subgroup generated by all values
`phi(L_i(beta))` over all assignments `phi:F_n -> H_prod` and all recursive
longitudes.  If every closed product label lies in that subgroup, then
identity `H_prod` longitude data kills the residual product action.  This
strictly generalizes the earlier one-longitude and per-coordinate witness
diagnostics.

## Exact all-n product target

Let `H_prod` be the finite totalized product-label group.  The needed product
lemma is:

> For every product local-minimal interval, for every braid degree `n`, base
> tuple `z`, coordinate `j`, and base-fixed braid `beta`, the closed label
> `h_{z,j}(beta)` is a finite product of evaluations of recursive Artin
> longitudes `L_i(beta)` under homomorphisms `F_n -> H_prod`, possibly after
> multiplying `H_prod` by the already-known cyclic, affine, coboundary, or
> known-branch detector factors.

The homomorphisms may depend on `n,z,j` and on the finite interval data, but
their target group must be the fixed finite group chosen from the interval,
not a group depending on `n`.

If this lemma holds, then

```text
Lambda_{H_prod,n}(beta)=Lambda_{H_prod,n}(1)
```

forces every factor in the displayed product to be identity, hence
`h_{z,j}(beta)=1` for all `z,j`.  The sharp obstruction theorem then gives
the interval detector `A_{H_prod}` or the corresponding product detector.

## Two invalid shortcuts

First, it is not enough that the product labels live in a finite groupoid.
A finite closed label can remain nontrivial on a base-fixed braid unless it
is shown to be controlled by Artin longitudes.  The one-colour commuting
swapped branch demonstrates this: coboundary telescoping fails, and the
residual label is detected only after using the cyclic abelian-longitude
criterion.

Second, it is not enough that the base tuple is fixed.  Base-fixing removes
colour motion, but the closed fibre label can still carry pairwise-linking or
holonomy information.  The product proof must kill that closed holonomy by
longitude evaluations in a fixed finite group, or a counterexample must make
such holonomy survive every finite-group longitude detector.

## Relation to outcome B

A product-branch counterexample would have to produce explicit product data
and braids `beta_j in B_{q_j}`, with `q_j -> infinity`, such that:

```text
Lambda_{G,q_j}(beta_j)=Lambda_{G,q_j}(1)
```

eventually for every finite group `G`, but some closed product label
`h_{z_j,k_j}(beta_j)` remains nontrivial in the fixed finite groupoid of the
interval.  This is exactly the normalized-law obstruction sequence specialized
to product labels.

Consequently, the genuinely coloured product branch is now reduced to one
precise A/B fork:

- prove the closed-label longitude-product lemma above; or
- construct a normalized-law sequence whose longitudes vanish in every finite
  group while a closed product label remains nontrivial.

The second implication is made formal in
`proofs/product_closed_label_obstruction.md`: once an explicit product
interval is proved to defeat every finite closed-label detector, the standard
finite-group direct-product diagonalization supplies the required
`q_j -> infinity` sequence.
