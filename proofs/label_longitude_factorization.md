# Label-longitude factorization

Date: 2026-05-28

This note records a branch-level sufficient condition for applying the
finite-longitude factorization criterion.  It is meant for product-label,
Green-corridor, and related finite groupoid-label branches.

## Statement

Let `pi : X -> Z` be a local interval over a quotient dominated by `Q`, and
let `N_n = ker rho_{Q,n}`.  Suppose a residual branch has a finite label
group `H`, independent of `n`, and for every braid degree `n`, base word
`z in Z^n`, coordinate `j`, and `beta in N_n`, the residual coordinate map is
obtained from a label element

```text
h_{z,j}(beta) in H.
```

Assume further that for each `(n,z,j)` there is a homomorphism

```text
phi_{z,j}: F_n -> H
```

such that

```text
h_{z,j}(beta) = phi_{z,j}(L_j(beta))
```

for the recursive Artin longitude `L_j(beta)`.  More generally, the same
argument works if `h_{z,j}(beta)` is a fixed finite product of evaluations of
the longitudes `L_i(beta)` under homomorphisms `F_n -> H`.

Then the finite group `H` detects this residual branch:

```text
Lambda_{H,n}(beta)=Lambda_{H,n}(1)  =>  h_{z,j}(beta)=1
```

for all `n,z,j,beta`, and hence the corresponding residual coordinate maps
are trivial.

## Proof

If `Lambda_{H,n}(beta)=Lambda_{H,n}(1)`, then the braid permutation is
trivial and every recursive Artin longitude evaluates to the identity under
every homomorphism `F_n -> H`.  In particular,

```text
phi_{z,j}(L_j(beta)) = 1
```

for every `(z,j)`.  Thus every label element `h_{z,j}(beta)` is identity.
If a coordinate map is a finite product of such longitude evaluations, each
factor is identity and the product is identity.  This is exactly the
finite-longitude factorization criterion with the finite group `G=H`.

## Product-Branch Translation

For a swapped product-permutation branch, the helper

```text
swapped_product_label_word_action(interval,z,beta)
```

returns a formal label word for each final coordinate.  When `beta in N_n`,
the base word is fixed; if the Artin permutation is also trivial, the
dependency vector is the identity.  The residual coordinate map is therefore
the evaluation of one formal label word in the finite coloured permutation
groupoid.

For a direct product-permutation branch, the dependency vector is always the
identity, and

```text
direct_product_label_word_action(interval,z,beta)
```

gives the corresponding coordinate label word.

Thus a product branch is closed by the following symbolic task:

> Find a finite label group `H`, built from the interval, and prove that every
> base-fixed product label word is a product of evaluations of recursive
> Artin longitudes in `H`.

Equivalently, for a fixed braid `beta`, every closed product label should lie
in the subgroup of `H` generated by all values `phi(L_i(beta))`, where
`phi:F_n -> H` ranges over all assignments and `i` ranges over the recursive
longitudes.  This longitude-value subgroup formulation is recorded in
`proofs/product_longitude_subgroup_criterion.md` and implemented by
`product_closed_label_longitude_subgroup_audit()`.  It is the correct
finite-product generalization of the older per-label witness diagnostic.

The finite group `H` can now be made canonical for this purpose.  The note
`proofs/product_label_group_detector.md` embeds the coloured product label
groupoid into the finite permutation group of the tagged fibre union
`disjoint union_a {a} x A_a`, by totalizing each partial label as a fibre
swap or within-fibre permutation.  The resulting `H_prod` is implemented by
`swapped_product_label_group()` and `direct_product_label_group()`, and tests
verify that evaluation in `H_prod` restricts to the original formal product
label evaluators.  The remaining theorem is not the existence of a finite
label group; it is the all-`n` proof that base-fixed words in this explicit
finite group are products of recursive Artin-longitude evaluations.

The already handled subbranches fit this template:

- coboundary labels evaluate to gauge transport and vanish on base-fixed
  braids;
- pairwise-linking labels are evaluations of abelianized longitudes in a
  finite cyclic group;
- one-colour local-minimal product labels reduce to a cyclic prime regular
  action;
- fibre-size-two product labels reduce to the affine `F_2` branch.

The genuinely coloured product-label theorem is precisely the missing
factorization of formal label words through finite Artin longitude
evaluations.

The product branch now has a gauge-normalized version of the same criterion.
Instead of working in the full tagged-fibre label group, one may first
normalize every label word to `g_t f g_s^{-1}` in the finite holonomy group
left by the coboundary audit.  The helper
`product_holonomy_longitude_subgroup_audit()` checks the corresponding
fixed-word longitude-value subgroup membership.  Proving that normalized
membership for all braid degrees is equivalent to closing the product
holonomy part, since the gauge part telescopes to identity on closed residual
paths.

## Green/Corridor Translation

The same condition is the desired bridge for the Green/corridor branch.
There the finite label group `H` should be assembled from:

- symmetric groups on two-sided Green kernel-block quotients;
- Schutzenberger permutation groups of regular Green classes;
- known-branch detector factors when a corridor collapses into an eliminated
  branch.

The open theorem-level assertion is that every residual corridor transport
and Green completed-context branch choice is a product of evaluations of
recursive Artin longitudes in this finite group `H`.

## Counterexample Consequence

A counterexample cannot merely produce a nontrivial finite label word.  It
must produce label words that are not forced by evaluations in any fixed
finite group `H`.  Equivalently, it must produce a normalized-law sequence
whose longitudes are eventually trivial in every finite `H`, while some
residual label word remains nontrivial.
