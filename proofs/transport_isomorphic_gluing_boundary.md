# Transport-Isomorphic Gluing Boundary

Date: 2026-06-04

This note records the current boundary after the Pro response to the
product-like transport question.

## Closed Boundary

Product-like mixed rows do not force transport by internal subsolution
isomorphisms.  The generated audit
`proofs/product_like_transport_boundary_audit.md` records the size-four
affine Type A model:

```text
X = F_2^2,
r((a,b),(c,d)) =
((d,a+b+d),(a+c+d+1,a+1)).
```

For its unique proper subsolution-fibre congruence, all mixed rows are
swapped-product-like bijections, but the two internal size-two block
solutions are not isomorphic.  Refining to singleton blocks removes the
obstruction only by destroying the proper fibre compression.  Thus product
likeness alone is genuinely weaker than product-like transport by internal
solution isomorphisms.

This is the finite answer to the earlier Question B: finite YBE locality does
not by itself upgrade product-like mixed transport to transport-isomorphic
mixed transport.

## Claimed Positive Route

The Pro answer claimed the following restricted positive theorem:

```text
If a proper YBE quotient has crossing-closed fibres, the quotient and all
fibres are rack-dominated, and every mixed row is product-like with
transport maps that are internal-solution isomorphisms, then X is
rack-dominated.
```

The proposed route is:

1. form the finite groupoid generated by the mixed transport isomorphisms;
2. choose gauges from each fibre to a representative fibre;
3. replace all mixed transports by identity to obtain a split solution `T`;
4. dominate `T` by the quotient rack and the internal block racks;
5. recover `X` from `T` by a finite sequential gauge, then apply the safe
   equivariant-reconstruction theorem.

This is a useful blueprint, but the repo should not promote it to a universal
theorem until the two certificate obligations below are discharged.

## Flatness Obligation

Transport-isomorphic mixed rows generate a finite groupoid of isomorphisms
between internal block solutions.  Choosing gauges along a spanning forest
normalizes the forest edges, but a loop in this groupoid can produce a
nontrivial automorphism of the representative block.

Thus simultaneous identity-normalization requires the loop monodromy groups

```text
M_a <= Aut(F_a)
```

to be trivial, or else the nontrivial loop action must be retained as part of
the finite detector/transducer state.  YBE cube coherence proves that
transport is functorial; it does not automatically prove that all functorial
loop automorphisms are identity.

The executable helper

```text
subsolution_fibre_transport_monodromy_audit(solution, partition)
```

therefore records a real gate, not cosmetic metadata.  Type B passes this
gate with trivial loop monodromy.  Type A does not even reach the gate,
because its product-like transports are not internal-solution isomorphisms.
The generated audit
`proofs/transport_isomorphic_gluing_boundary_audit.md` gives a concrete
six-point transport-isomorphic product-like solution with loop monodromy of
order `3`, so the flatness issue is not merely hypothetical.  The same audit
also records that the local router closes this row as
`product_finite_g_branch` with detector group order `3`; thus the intended
repair is not to ignore monodromy, but to make the finite monodromy detector
part of the marked factor.

## Split-Tower Obligation

Even after a split or normalized transport model `T` is built, the domination
of `T` by quotient and block rack detectors is a separate marked-factor
claim.  The safe theorem in
`proofs/ybe_equivariant_reconstruction_closure.md` requires total
`B_n`-equivariant marked factors whose product reconstructs the target in
every arity.

The Pro proof sketches the following decomposition:

```text
pure quotient-trivial braid on T
  -> delete all strands not carrying colour a
  -> internal braid beta_a on the a-coloured substrands.
```

That argument is valid in the flip-across/permutation-colour case used for
the observed Type B examples.  For a general quotient YBE solution, quotient
colours can change along the braid trajectory and return only at the end; in
that setting, a fixed `a`-coloured substrand braid is not automatic.

Consequently, the current safe positive statement is certificate-level:

```text
If the transport-isomorphic block data produce a finite split marked tower T,
and T is dominated by a finite product of quotient, block, and any necessary
monodromy detectors, and there is an all-arity injective B_n-equivariant
sequential code X^n -> T_n, then X is rack-dominated.
```

This follows immediately from the equivariant reconstruction closure theorem.
What remains to upgrade the Pro blueprint to the stated restricted theorem is
the split-tower domination lemma, possibly with explicit monodromy
rackification.

## Current Use

The boundary is now:

- Type A is closed by the separate parity/fibre cyclic-rack gauge recorded in
  `proofs/affine_f2_hidden_cyclic_gauge.md` and the finite transducer
  certificate note.
- Type B is closed by flip-across domination; its product-like transports are
  transport-isomorphic with trivial transport loop monodromy.
- The broad transport-isomorphic gluing theorem remains a precise next lemma,
  not yet a repo-promoted proof, unless the flatness and split-tower
  obligations are proven.
