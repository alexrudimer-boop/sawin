# Green holonomy factorization gate

Date: 2026-05-28

This note adds one more guardrail to the Green/corridor branch.  It is not a
proof of the Master Local-Minimal Residual Theorem.  It identifies the exact
finite-depth group component that a hidden completed-context corridor must
enter before it can be relevant to a Sawin residual obstruction.

## Bounded completed-context loops

Fix a regular Green `R`-class `C` in a coordinate-action monoid and the
retained edge-germ audit from `proofs/green_branch_audit.md`.  For a depth
`d`, the bounded completed-context category has finite objects

```text
W_d(s) = retained context words of length <= d ending at s in C.
```

The completed YBE rows give partial maps between these finite word sets.  The
atom relation records the quotient already forced by local branch choices.
A loop is atom-trivial if it fixes every atom word.

Raw atom-trivial loops split into two kinds.

- A reset-like loop collapses or partially forgets finite context words.
- A group-like loop is a total bijection of `W_d(s)`.

Only the second kind can be a finite-stage shadow of a residual braid action:
each `delta_{n,z}(beta)` is a permutation of the finite fibre `X_z`.

## Gate lemma

Lemma.  In any finite completed-context observer, a nontrivial residual
motion that is atom-trivial at depth `d` must define a nonidentity element of
the finite permutation group generated by the total bijective atom-trivial
loops on some `W_d(s)`.

Proof.  A residual braid action is a bijection.  Therefore any finite
observer through which it factors must act on the observed finite fibre by a
permutation.  Atom-trivial reset maps, undefined maps, and non-surjective
maps cannot be the observed action of a residual braid element; at most they
record loss of information in the observer.  The remaining atom-trivial
finite maps are total bijections of the word fibre, hence permutations.  The
finite group generated by all such loops contains the observed permutation.
If that observed permutation is outside the identity, the group has a
nonidentity element.  If the generated transition monoid were purely
aperiodic, the aperiodic permutation lemma of
`proofs/finite_semigroup_holonomy_route.md` would force every such
permutation to be the identity.  Thus nontrivial residual motion must enter
the group part, not the aperiodic reset part.

The unit-holonomy longitude gate in
`proofs/unit_holonomy_longitude_gate.md` adds the finite-G detection step for
such group parts.  Once the relevant completed-context monoid is fixed, its
total bijective loops form a finite unit group `U(M)`.  A group-like corridor
label is killed by finite-longitude data whenever it lies in
`V_beta(U(M))`, the subgroup generated by all recursive longitude values in
that unit group.  Thus the required symbolic bridge is not merely "find a
group-like loop"; it is "prove the loop belongs to the appropriate
longitude-value subgroup of a fixed unit group."

## Consequence for A

The all-`n` Green/corridor bridge may now be stated more sharply.

For a local-minimal bi-free universal-corridor interval, it is enough to
prove that every group-like atom-trivial completed-context loop arising from
a residual braid action maps, compatibly in depth and braid index, into the
fixed detector product

```text
H(pi,Q) =
  two-sided symmetric Green kernel-block groups
  x two-sided Schutzenberger groups
  x known branch detector factors
  x quotient detector factors from Q.
```

The group `H(pi,Q)` is finite and depends only on the interval and the
quotient detector, not on `n`.  Once recursive Artin-longitude data in this
group is trivial, the group-like corridor part is trivial.  The preceding
lemma and the finite semigroup aperiodic lemma then rule out any leftover
reset-like residual motion.

This formulation does not remove the main theorem burden.  The missing step
is still the uniform symbolic factorization:

```text
residual Green/corridor group-like loop
    = finite product of input-dependent recursive Artin-longitude evaluations
      in H(pi,Q),
```

for every braid index and every residual input tuple.

## Consequence for B

A counterexample through the Green/corridor route cannot be certified by
showing only that bounded completed-context observers have atom-trivial
collapses.  Those may be aperiodic or non-bijective artifacts of the observer.
A B construction must instead supply a compatible sequence of nonidentity
group-like atom-trivial loops whose associated residual tuple motion is
invisible to every fixed finite group detector by normalized laws.

Equivalently, the B-route must escape the fixed finite Green/Schutzenberger
detector product in the group part.  A pure reset corridor is not enough.

## Executable layer

The helper

```text
bounded_atom_trivial_loop_group_summaries(audit, max_depth)
```

extracts, for each finite Green source `s`, the finite permutation group
generated by total bijective atom-trivial loops on `W_d(s)`.  It deliberately
ignores non-bijective atom-trivial loops, because those cannot be residual
permutation motion.

The regression tests record two guardrails:

- the size-three affine commutator candidate has trivial bounded
atom-trivial loop groups at depth `2`, matching the absence of hidden
completed-context loops in that row;
- the two-point identity table has raw atom-trivial context collapses, but
  these are reset-like and produce only the trivial bounded loop group.

The lower-level helper
`unit_longitude_subgroup_audit(monoid,n,beta,labels)` then checks, for an
explicit finite monoid observer and explicit unit labels, whether those labels
lie in the corresponding longitude-value subgroup.

These tests are finite-depth diagnostics only.  They are useful because they
force future A or B arguments to talk about group-like holonomy rather than
about raw context collapse.
