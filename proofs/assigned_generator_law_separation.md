# Assigned-generator law separation

Date: 2026-05-29

This note isolates the exact finite diagnostic used by the normalized-law B
route.  It is not a counterexample and it is not a finite-search proof.

## Criterion

Let `G_1,...,G_r` be finite detector groups and let

```text
p_1,...,p_k in Sym(S)
```

be a specific tuple of permutation images, for example the restrictions of
standard pure braid generators to one structure orbit.  A finite diagnostic
word `w` is useful for B only when both conditions hold:

```text
w is a law on every G_i,
w(p_1,...,p_k) != 1 in Sym(S).
```

The second condition is assigned-generator nontriviality.  It is sharper than
saying that `w` is not a law on the whole group generated by the `p_i`, since
the actual embedded braid evaluates `w` on this particular tuple.

## Relation To Normalized Laws

If the detector list contains all finite groups of size at most `j`, and the
word `w_j` satisfies the two conditions above for a braid index `q_j`, then
the pure-braid law embedding gives a braid `beta_j` whose finite-group
Artin-longitude data is identity for every group in that list, while the
assigned-generator evaluation moves a tuple.

To become outcome B, this must be promoted to a symbolic sequence with
`q_j -> infinity` and with the detector list exhausting all finite groups.
The helper below only supplies fixed finite stages.

## Executable Helper

The helper

```text
short_law_separating_permutation_assignment(law_groups, generator_images, max_length=...)
```

searches reduced free words and returns an `AssignedLawSeparation` containing:

- the arity;
- the target permutation degree;
- a separating word, if found;
- the evaluated permutation on the assigned tuple;
- one moved index.

The structure-orbit helper

```text
structure_orbit_law_separation(X,n,law_groups,max_length=...)
```

now delegates its assigned pure-generator search to this helper.  The moved
index is then translated back into an explicit moved tuple inside the chosen
structure orbit.

## Guardrail

For racks and other already dominated branches, assigned-generator separators
may appear if the detector list is too small.  They disappear when the correct
fixed detector, such as the rack inner group, is included.  Therefore a B
proof must use an increasing all-finite-group law list, not a convenient
small list.

The regression fixture separates `C_2,C_3` from the adjacent transposition
assignment in `S_3` by the commutator word.  This is only a convention check:
it is deliberately the same finite phenomenon that disappears once `S_3` is
included as a detector.
